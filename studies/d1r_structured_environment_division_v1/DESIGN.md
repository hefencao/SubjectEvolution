# Design

## Failure of the previous ordering

D1-Q attempted to qualify equilibrium before the environment could generate
structured social dependence, and the reported formal panel had no passing
seed. Continuing whole-genome retention analysis would therefore measure a
poorly structured environment rather than reveal a mature subject.

D1-R reverses the order:

1. construct plural physical opportunities and dependencies;
2. verify viable multi-generation operation;
3. require repeated group-internal functional differentiation across seeds;
4. only in a later version freeze the environment and design any genetic study.

## Role-neutral physical network

The intervention changes no inherited coordinate, mutation rule, maintenance
cost or reproduction rule. It adds:

- four periodic source provinces with different centers, radii and forcing;
- four processing provinces spatially offset from source provinces;
- four complementary recipes, each consuming at least two raw channels;
- bounded raw-resource exchange using only donor surplus and receiver capacity;
- exchange-supported, uniformly decaying relations for the pre-existing group
  detector;
- observational fixed-window records of harvesting, processing, exchange,
  signalling and social movement.

The diagnostic does not name occupations. A division candidate must have a
stable six-member core, high participation, multidimensional and specialized
member activity, multiple active recipes, real internal raw exchange, and a
minimum dependence on exchanged material. It must persist for at least two
windows. Formal environment maturity additionally requires at least two such
persistent groups in every independent seed.

## Evidence classes

- A single probe may reject bad parameter regions or expose broken wiring.
- A probe can never establish environment plurality.
- Three independent formal seeds are required for the environment threshold.
- Passing that threshold does not establish adaptation, selection, fixed social
  roles, subjecthood or permission for a single-round genetic audit.
